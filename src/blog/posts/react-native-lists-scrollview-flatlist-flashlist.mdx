## What I measured

| | Rows rendered | Views | Mounts after scrolling 1,000 rows | Time to switch to it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ScrollView | 10,000 | 60,000 | none: all 10,000 mounted up front | 276–391 ms |
| FlatList | 63 at the top, 119 mid-list | 379–715 | about 1,050 | 33–38 ms |
| FlashList | 18 | 109 | 0 (still the first 18) | 2–8 ms |

The times will differ on your machine and much more on a phone. What carries over is the shape: ScrollView's cost
grows with the length of the list, FlatList's grows with how far you scroll, and FlashList's stays flat.

## ScrollView: everything, up front

A ScrollView doesn't know it holds a list. It renders every child you pass it, on screen or not, and keeps all of
them mounted. With 10,000 rows of six views each, that's 60,000 views before the user sees anything.

That's fine for a settings screen, a form or a list you know stays short. It's the wrong tool for anything that can
grow, such as a feed, search results or a long checklist. The cost of the first render grows with every item, and
all of it runs on the JS thread while the screen waits.

## FlatList: a window around the screen

FlatList virtualizes. By default, `windowSize` is 21: the visible screen plus 10 screens above and 10 below. Rows
outside the window aren't rendered. In the demo that's 63 rows at the top of the list, where there's nothing above,
and about 119 in the middle.

As you scroll, the window moves. Rows that leave it unmount, and rows that enter it mount from scratch. Scrolling
1,000 rows down mounted about 1,050 rows in the demo: roughly one new row for every row scrolled past. Each of those
mounts is a full render of the row on the JS thread.

That's where FlatList's familiar problems come from:

- **Blank rows when scrolling fast.** If you scroll faster than the JS thread can mount rows, you reach parts of the
  list that haven't rendered yet. (If the JS thread is busy with something else, it's worse; [why Reanimated
  exists](/blog/reanimated-js-thread-ui-thread) shows what a blocked JS thread does.)
- **Work that grows with scrolling.** A long session in a feed mounts thousands of rows, each paying the full cost of
  its first render.

The real FlatList also renders in batches (`initialNumToRender` is 10 by default, then `maxToRenderPerBatch` rows at a
time), so its first screen appears faster than the demo's, and the rest of the window fills in after. The demo
renders the whole window at once, which makes the window's size easier to see.

## FlashList: a pool of rows, reused

FlashList renders a little past the edges of the screen, then stops creating rows. When a row scrolls off one end, it
gets the data for the row coming on at the other end and is moved there. Its views stay; only its props change.

In the demo the pool is 18 rows, and that's still the number mounted after scrolling 1,000 rows or all 10,000.
Updating an existing row with new data is much cheaper than mounting a new one, which is why FlashList scrolls
smoothly through lists that make FlatList show blank space.

Two things come with recycling:

- **Rows of different shapes need their own pools.** If a header gets recycled into a photo row, almost everything in
  it has to change. FlashList's `getItemType` keeps a pool per type.
- **Local state moves with the row.** A recycled row keeps its `useState` values, so state from one item can show up
  on another. FlashList v2's `useRecyclingState` resets it when the item changes.

I covered both, and the rest of a real migration, in [moving long checklists from FlatList to FlashList
v2](/blog/flashlist-checklist-scroll-jank). FlashList v2 runs on the New Architecture only.

## Which one to use

| Your content | Use |
| --- | --- |
| A screen of fixed content, a form, a list that stays at a few dozen short rows | ScrollView |
| A long list where the rows are cheap and you'd rather not add a dependency | FlatList, with its props tuned |
| A long or growing list, heavy rows, or rows of different kinds | FlashList |

Whichever you use, keep each row cheap to render, and judge scrolling in a release build. Development builds are
much slower and will make any list look worse than it is.

## FAQ

### Is FlatList bad?

No. FlatList's window is a big improvement over a ScrollView, and for many lists it's enough. It mounts a new row for
every row you scroll past, so it struggles when rows are heavy or users scroll fast through long lists. That's where
FlashList's recycling helps.

### Why does FlashList render so few rows?

It only needs enough rows to cover the screen plus a small margin past each edge. Instead of creating more as you
scroll, it reuses the ones that left the screen. The demo uses a 250 px margin, which needs 18 rows of 52 px.

### Should I put a FlatList inside a ScrollView?

Avoid it. A FlatList inside a vertical ScrollView can't tell which of its rows are on screen, so it loses its
virtualization and renders more than it should. Use the list's `ListHeaderComponent` and `ListFooterComponent` for
content above and below it instead.
